# Fixed contacts, finite-order recovery and competing components

The following is the ordinary Morse–Bott/Laplace argument specialized to the
56-real-dimensional eight-mode Gaussian manifold. It is not claimed as a new
abstract Laplace theorem. Its use requires actual global contacts and positive
normal curvature; a collection of local numerical maxima is insufficient.

Let f(g)=-log(|<w,g>|²/h²), and suppose its global zero set is a finite union
of compact smooth contact components C_i of dimensions s_i. Suppose f is C4
in tubular neighborhoods, its normal Hessian is positive definite uniformly,
and it is bounded below by delta>0 outside those neighborhoods. Compactness,
smooth Haar density and a finite atlas then give

    Z_k^(i) = h^(2k) k^(-(56-s_i)/2) (A_i+O(1/k)),
    A_i=(2 pi)^((56-s_i)/2) int_(C_i) rho/sqrt(det H_normal).

The precise density convention is the same in all A_i. The dimension factor
d_(8,k) has degree 28, so q_k is a sum of terms proportional to h^(2k) k^(s_i/2).
For components of different dimension, the largest dimension dominates a
FIXED witness unless its coefficient vanishes (which positive smooth Haar
density excludes here). The normal RMS width is O(k^(-1/2)).

For a finite-k bound, assume a verified tube radius r0, inequalities
c d_perp² <= f <= C d_perp² in the tube, and positive lower/upper density
bounds. Gaussian integration gives an explicit Z_k/h^(2k) lower bound
c0 k^(-(56-smax)/2) for k>=k0. Hence, for rho<=r0,

    P_k{d(g,C)>rho} <= (1/c0) k^((56-smax)/2)
       [exp(-k c rho²)+exp(-k delta)].

Constants can be sharpened using radial volume integration. The same argument
applies to coefficient mass Lambda_k, whose exponent is 2k-1. This establishes
a conditional finite-order support-recovery statement with clearly specified
inputs. No global numerical c,delta,r0 have yet been certified for FK.

## Moving canonical directions

Suppose smooth nearby stationary components persist under w_k, their local
weights tend to positive A_i, and canonical reconstruction forces limiting
coefficient-mass ratios m_i/m_j>0. Assuming all neglected components and
remainders are controlled, coefficient mass obeys

    log(m_i/m_j) = log(A_i/A_j)
      +(2k-1)log(h_i/h_j)+(s_i-s_j)log(k)/2+o(1).

Thus a log(k)/k height adjustment is possible when the dimensions differ.
If they agree, the leading correction is instead O(1/k), determined by
log(m_i/m_j)-log(A_i/A_j). This is a conditional implication, not a premise
about FK's contact set or an unconditional claim about its canonical path.

For the t20 two-orbit candidate, each exhibited orbit is S6. Their covariance
derivatives give the same induced metric (1-a²) times the unit-sphere metric.
Their 50 normal curvatures agree to 40+ digits in independent precision runs.
Conditioned on complete global classification and nondegeneracy, these facts
predict equal limiting weights for the FIXED candidate. The physical target's
two-orbit construction has highly unequal masses. Its MOVING canonical
directions would therefore need an O(1/k) height imbalance even though both
contact components have equal dimension. Global completeness is still open.

TWO_CONTACT_TOY.md supplies a separate known-optimum two-component example
with exact integer moments. There the equal dimensions, equal local weights,
unequal target coefficients and 1/k direction adjustment can be checked
without assuming the unproved FK matching-dual certificate.

## Soft directions limit what k<=128 can resolve

For the frozen t9/t11 candidates, algebraically stationary representatives
remove the previous ascent residual. At 50 and 90 decimal digits their six
small positive curvatures are approximately 7.119525e-6 and 6.668687e-6;
six other curvatures vanish by diagonal Spin(7) symmetry. A quadratic normal
direction is tightly confined only once k times its curvature is large.
These witnesses therefore cannot be expected to exhibit their ultimate
six-dimensional local scaling at k<=128. This is a reason to analyze the
softness, not to raise k to 256. Their global support remains unproved.

## Exact soft eigenvalues, and sensitivity of the frozen witness

For a real singlet witness y, put z=y2/sqrt7,
C=(y0+z)/2, D=(-y0+5z)/4. An interior stationary A(a,n) has
y1=2 sqrt2 D a and h=C-D a². At n=e0 take the six relative cluster rotations
generated by -i(Q_A(0j)-Q_B(0j))/2, j=1,...,6. Exact Clifford/polynomial
calculation gives orthonormal horizontal tangent vectors and

    D_scaled† G_j g=0,
    D_scaled† G_j² g=(0,2a,-4).

The generalized Hessian identity against every one of the 120 Majorana
generator directions gives the eigenvalue, not only a Rayleigh quotient:

    kappa_relative=4(z-D a²)/(C-D a²), multiplicity at least 6.

`exact_relative_rotation_hessian` checks all 720 identities with exact dyadic
polynomials after b²=1-a². No fitted coefficient enters this statement.
Directed 80-digit evaluation certifies strictly positive eigenvalues for the
exact binary64 y coefficients of the frozen t9/t11 candidates. Their intervals
are checked against the independent 90-digit full metric spectrum.

These small values are SENSITIVE TO THE WITNESS. The zero-curvature surface is
y1²+2 y0 z-10 z²=0. Holding y0,y1 fixed, changing y2 by about -2.13966e-6
at t9, or -1.76500e-6 at t11, reaches that surface. Therefore the soft modes
are real for the frozen candidate, but may reflect its small deviation from
a degenerate optimal-witness surface. The current evidence does not locate
the exact global optimum or prove that a zero Hessian direction integrates
into an additional contact manifold. No physical phase transition is claimed.
